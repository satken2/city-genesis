# 6. Real estate market

## 6.1. Space and occupancy

Households and facilities without their own buildings rent space in shared buildings. Space is classified by uses such as residential, retail, and office; tenants need space suitable for their activity. In multi-tenant buildings, each tenant rents the area it needs. Some buildings, such as detached houses, are occupied entirely by one household or facility.

Insufficient space of the required kind in a district encourages new [buildings](05_buildings.md).

## 6.2. Rent

Tenants pay rent to buildings for the use of space. A building's `rent` is the amount per day per square meter. Rents rise when many agents want to move in and fall when vacancies are high. Market rents affect land values and the profitability of new construction. Households unable to pay rent must find another home.

## 6.3. Location and relocation

Facilities consider rent and business suitability; households also consider commuting, education, shopping access, and the living environment. Building pauses and redevelopment can also trigger relocation.

Households and facilities without premises temporarily occupy a virtual building in each district representing agents awaiting relocation. This means they have not secured residential or business space. Households evicted by a building pause, demolition, or unpaid rent move there immediately and search for a new home from there. Private shops, factories, and offices in a building scheduled for demolition close instead of relocating. See [households](07_households.md) for the distinction between immigrants awaiting housing and established residents.

## 6.4. Spontaneous urban sprawl

Persistent housing shortages and hardship cause unpaved alleys, small parcels, and slum buildings to appear independently of city plans. This occurs when road and residential development fall behind demand, leaving households unable to obtain formal housing.

Alleys extend from existing roads and attract small homes. They bypass normal construction plans and building regulations, producing development that differs from the player's intended land use. Sprawl stops expanding when housing demand is met and hardship subsides.

These alleys and buildings become part of the city; players can rebuild or remove their roads. Slum buildings may be replaced by ordinary buildings when financially viable. Planned housing and road development helps residents obtain formal homes and supports prevention and redevelopment of sprawl.
