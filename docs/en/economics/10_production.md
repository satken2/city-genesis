# 10. Production

## 10.1. What supports production

Standard facilities use labor and materials to produce goods and services. Insufficient space, workers, or materials constrains production. Industries requiring advanced skills also need an educated workforce ([employment and education](08_employment-and-education.md)).

Private facilities adjust production to demand and sales. Unsold output encourages contraction; growing demand encourages higher production, more space, and new entrants. Persistent unprofitability leads to exit ([agents](02_agents.md)). `utilization` measures how much productive capacity is used; `profit_rates` describes profitability. It measures the profitability of production and excludes taxes, dividends, and transfers with the central bank.

## 10.2. Public facility operation

Some public facilities follow demand, while others maintain spare capacity for fluctuations or emergencies. Electricity and water must meet daily demand reliably; fire and police services need readiness even when ordinary use is low. Players adjust operating scale through budgets and utilization targets. See [budgets](15_budgets.md).

## 10.3. Prices

Private prices reflect materials, labor, rent, and margins responding to sales conditions. Higher efficiency reduces costs; shortages and unsold output affect prices.

For public services such as healthcare, electricity, and water, the mayor sets a fixed price or a ratio to cost. Services can also be free. The difference between operating costs and revenue affects city government finances.

## 10.4. Location, equipment, and climate

Foot traffic, industry clusters, and reputation built through long operation improve a facility's attractiveness or business conditions. Equipment investment raises production efficiency, but equipment depreciates over time.

Solar power, wind power, and agriculture respond to sunshine, wind speed, temperature, and precipitation. Simulation uses the city's climate for each epoch, a separate state from the weather being rendered ([weather and lighting](../geometries/U_weather-and-lighting.md)).
