# 12. Financial system

## 12.1. Money circulation and borrowing

Each agent has a cash balance (`reserves`). Payments transfer the same amount from payer to recipient. Transactions within the city do not change its total money.

A negative balance represents borrowing. Private businesses can borrow to start operations or construct buildings, within limits determined by collateral and the size of the city's economy. City growth enables investment in larger factories and buildings.

## 12.2. Central bank

The central bank assumes assets and liabilities left by private agents that withdraw. These balances are returned to or shared across the economy over time, preventing large failures from imposing their full burden immediately.

Money associated with land settlement and sales of city-owned land to private owners also returns to the economy through the central bank. Public facility funds are consolidated in city government accounts ([budgets](15_budgets.md)).

## 12.3. Money flows across the city boundary

The city is one region sharing a currency with the outside world and accepts externally determined trade prices. Exports bring money in; imports take it out. Tourists' travel funds are also external income.

The city's total money changes through its trade balance and quest grants. City government and the central bank do not automatically offset outflows from a trade deficit. See [product market](09_product-market.md) and [tourism](20_tourism.md).

## 12.4. Economic size and prices

| Indicator | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Nominal GDP (`gdp`) | Value added produced by the city at current prices |
| Price index (`price_index`) | Local output prices relative to external prices |
| Real GDP (`real_gdp`) | Economic size excluding price-level effects |

Separating nominal and real GDP distinguishes growth in production from rising prices.
